## Identify the level before interpreting a state

| Question | Where to look | What not to conclude |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was the request processed correctly? | `meta.status` and the endpoint's response contract. | That payment was approved. |
| What happened to this attempt? | `transaction.state` in authorization/callback, or `operation.transactions[].state` in a query. | That every attempt has this outcome. |
| What is the operation's situation? | `operation.state`, when included. | That it always matches the last transaction received by your system. |
| Has money reached the merchant bank account? | The relevant settlement and payout information. | That `AUTORIZADO` means deposited funds. |

<Warning>
  **HTTP 200, `success: true` or `meta.status.code: "00"` are not enough to mark an order as paid.** API examples show QR generation succeeding while the transaction remains `PENDIENTE`.
</Warning>

## Current states and recommended actions

These nine states apply to the operation and transaction model. The table explains their meaning for a **transaction**. An operation state describes the grouped purchase, not necessarily every attempt.

| Current state | Meaning for the attempt | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Registered** | The attempt has a record. | Keep its identity. Registration is not proof of payment. |
| **Pending** | No final payment confirmation yet. A QR, CIP or continuation URL may exist. | Do not confirm the purchase based on this attempt. Wait for notification or query from your backend. |
| **Authorized** | The attempt was authorized. | Validate context, amount and currency before applying paid-order logic. Do not confuse it with settlement or merchant payout. |
| **Invalid** | The attempt could not complete validly. | Review the reason, fix the issue or contact support. Do not blindly repeat the request. |
| **Declined** | The issuer or applicable rules rejected the attempt. | Record the decline and check whether another attempt is appropriate within the operation. |
| **Voided** | A previous authorization was reversed. | Preserve its history and reconcile the reversal. |
| **Settled** | The payment advanced to settlement processing. | Use for tracking and reconciliation; do not automatically equate it with a bank-account deposit. |
| **Expired** | The attempt's validity ended. | Verify its current outcome before offering another option. Do not rely solely on a browser timer. |
| **Cancelled** | The attempt was cancelled. | Identify which resource was cancelled. Closing a window is not a platform-confirmed cancellation. |

### Business labels and API values

Published API examples include `REGISTRADO`, `PENDIENTE`, `AUTORIZADO`, `DENEGADO`, `INVALIDO`, `CANCELADO` and `EXTORNADO`. Preserve the literal value specified by your contract; **do not translate these values into English in code**.

This business-state list does not replace an endpoint or notification schema. A service can report only a subset. Preserve unknown values and confirm their meaning; never classify them as approved by default.

## One transaction, several state changes

A documented authorization journey is:

<div className="pc-lifecycle not-prose"><span>REGISTRADO</span><b aria-hidden="true">→</b><span>PENDIENTE</span><b aria-hidden="true">→</b><span className="pc-ok">AUTORIZADO</span></div>

These events belong to the **same transaction_id**, not three attempts. A new attempt has its own identity and history within the operation.

Not every transaction follows this path. An attempt may never authorize; an authorization may receive a later adjustment. Do not treat this diagram as a mandatory sequence for all methods.

## Confirm outcomes without duplicating actions

<Steps>
  <Step title="Connect the response to the purchase">Identify merchant, operation and transaction_id. Check the amount and currency against the expected order.</Step>
  <Step title="Read the attempt outcome">Read the correct state field for the response type. Use state_reason and processor details for diagnostics, not as substitutes for state.</Step>
  <Step title="Verify from your backend">Query the outcome or process server notifications according to your integration. A visual checkout response is not the final source on its own.</Step>
  <Step title="Update idempotently">Keep per-transaction state and operation context. Processing an event again must not create another sale, delivery or confirmation email.</Step>
</Steps>

### When the information is insufficient

- **Timeout or interrupted network:** unknown does not mean declined. Query before starting another charge.
- **Repeated notification:** identify the attempt and event; do not repeat an action already applied.
- **Late response from another attempt:** do not overwrite the purchase outcome based solely on arrival order.
- **Conflicting states:** query again and record the case for review. Do not fulfill an order by inference.
- **Sensitive technical message:** show customers a safe explanation. Keep technical details in authorized support channels.

## Common misunderstandings

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="The service returned 00 and a CIP. Can I fulfill the order?">Not based on that alone. The CIP lets the customer continue payment, but the transaction can remain pending. Wait for or query confirmation.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="One transaction is authorized and another declined. What should I store?">Both, each under its transaction_id. Keep their states separate and validate the operation's situation. Do not delete the decline or let its late arrival automatically mark the purchase as failed.</Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do authorized, settled and merchant payout mean the same thing?">No. These are different moments. Authorization alone does not demonstrate settlement or a bank-account deposit.</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
